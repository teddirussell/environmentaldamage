Letter exchange

Meeting

18–19 May 2026 — John Brice to Troy Anderson (Director Civil Services) & Paul Crozier, Ballina Shire Council, cc Teddi Russell

Troy

As you know I met with Essential Energy Friday 8th May. I believe I imparted the seriousness of the issues at hand. I went well prepared and I believe I was well received. I gave them a few options to consider. I have asked for another face-to-face meeting within the next 2 weeks. I am expecting a commercial outcome. If for whatever the reason that does not happen, I will have to revert to Council as the keeper of the Dedicated Drainage Easement.

Troy Anderson replied asking for clarity on the intended purpose of a meeting with Council.

Troy

This is very simple, John Truman over a 5-year span, simply said "My road my Problem", hoping I would go away. As you would now realise, I will not be that guy and go away. By the way the law puts this as the Councils Road and it is a council Problem. Law however in this case puts the offender as Essential Energy's Predecessor.

There is a real chance this will end up with me lodging formal complaints with all that will listen. If this becomes a reality the owners can stand their ground and only give access to Essential Energy conditionally upon:

  1. An environmental Impact Study (EIS) conducted on Flora, Fauna and Soil. Quote estimate of $150,000.00 to be paid by EE.
  2. A seasonal Hydraulics Study conducted over the next 12 months. Quote estimate $250,000.00 to be paid for by EE.
  3. Removal of the road. If Toxic waste as all assume it is: quote of $1.3 million to $1.7 million. If not toxic: quote of $325,000.00.
  4. Return the three Agriculture drains to the way they were in 1986, before the road was added. Estimate of $100,000.00.
  5. 5-year rejuvenation plan for the 42 acres as suggested by the EPA, estimated at a minimum of $700,000.00 to $2,000,000.00 depending on the results of the EIS.

I have put to EE 4 options. All except the first governed by complete confidentiality.

  1. Do nothing, and all the above will be imposed, plus Mike Ahearn (lot 21) added into the loop — any cost projection above multiplied by 2.5, as lot 21's easement is 470 metres long.
  2. Put in new Ardill Payne culverts x3, estimated cost $60,000.00. Add an agreed maintenance program that the property owners and Council can sign off on. Supply the equipment needed and the cost of running and maintenance for a 5-year rehabilitation project to EPA guidelines matching Council's and EE's Biodiversity Protocols.
  3. Heron Todd White valuation of the lower 38–40 acres, subdivided and sold to EE.
  4. Ground Zero — executive decision. Install the 3 culverts as above, maintenance program approved by Council, plus a commercial $ amount paid for loss of opportunity to the landowner, also carried out by Heron Todd White.

Under your own Council Procedures and Protocols, I am asking for a meeting to discuss.

John Brice, Chief Operating Officer (COO)
For and on behalf of Theodora Andrea Brice and Shane Russell
